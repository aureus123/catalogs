1854+132811, Aquilae, 5 mag. and greenish white, with a faint companion 9.2 mag., distance 17.4″, angle of position 252°. Discovered by W. Herschel, 25 June, 1781. An optical double star.
1859-0413h Aquilae, two stars 6 mag., distance 36″, recognised as a double star by Bradley.
1901+0803R Aquilae, a variable star of a deep red colour. At maximum it is sometimes 6.5 mag., at minimum 11 mag. Period 345 days.
1903+321917 Lyrae, (Σ 2461), a yellowish star 6 mag., with a companion 10 mag. d = 3.7″, p = 324°.
1903+04034470, Star Cluster in Aquila, fairly large, rich, somewhat dense, diameter 12′ to 15′. Discovered by W. Herschel, 30 July, 1785.
1910+3857η Lyrae, (Σ 2487), a blueish star 4.5 mag., with a companion, d = 28″, p = 84°.
1911-01084482, Star Cluster in Aquila, consisting of stars from 9 to 12 mag., discovered by W. Herschel.
1912+29584485, Star Cluster in Lyra. Discovered by Messier 23 January, 1779, as a starless nebula in Lyra. In 1784 Herschel resolved it into stars. It appears dull in a 3½-inch refractor; 4′ to 5′ in diameter.
1912+3755θ Lyrae, 4.5 mag., with a companion 8.5 mag., d = 101″, p = 70°.
1912+005223, Aquilae (Σ 2492), 6 mag., yellowish, with a faint companion 9.5 mag., d = 3.4″, p = 11°.
1913+06204487, Nebula in Aquila, fairly large, round, of moderate brilliancy. According to Rosse it is of spiral arrangement.
1916+1857Σ 2504, Vulpeculae, 6.5 mag., with a companion 8 mag., d = 9″.
1924+24256 Vulpeculae, appearing to the naked eye like a star of 4 mag. In reality 2 stars of 4 and 5 mag., d = 403″.
1926+2742β Cygni, a reddish yellow star 3 mag., with a blue companion 5 mag., distance 34″. The primary is variable, but the variations of brightness are not important; the colour also varies slightly.
1931+1612ε Sagittae, 6 mag., with a companion 7.5 mag., d = 92″, p = 81°.
1934+4956R Cygni, of a deep red colour, at maximum 6 mag., at minimum very faint (3 mag.) Recognised as variable by Pogson in 1852. The period is, at least, 417 days. [Reported Aug. 13, 1888, to have a remarkably bright line (apparently F) in its spectrum, thus indicating a change in its constitution since Dunér's observations in 1879—80 and 1882.—Edit.]
1934-163454, Sagittarii, 6 mag., with a companion 7.5 mag., d = 45″, p = 42°, and another very faint companion, discovered by Burnham, d = 36″, p = 245°.
1936+26324508, Star Cluster in Vulpecula, very large, fairly rich in stars, not very dense. The stars range from 9.5 to 12 mag.
1937+1132χ Aquilae, 6 mag., with a companion 7 mag., distance only 0.6″.
1937-14264510, Nebula in Sagittarius, a planetary nebula, discovered by Herschel 8 Aug., 1787. He describes it as small, decidedly nebulous at the edges, and of uniform brightness, 10″ to 15″ in diameter. Several small stars are visible near to it. The spectrum contains 3 bright lines.
