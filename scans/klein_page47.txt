1757-2935W Sagittarii, recognised as variable by J. Schmidt in 1866. At maximum it is 5, at minimum 6.5 mag. The period seems to be from 7 to 8 days.
1759+66384373, Nebula in Draco. Discovered by Herschel, 15 Feb., 1786. He describes it as a planetary nebula of great brilliancy, disk 35″ in diameter, one angle very ill-defined. A long and careful examination reveals a brilliant, well-defined centre, round in shape. The nebula can be seen very well with a 3½-inch refractor. It gives a beautiful spectrum of bright lines. Huggins found the 3rd line the faintest. Vogel, on the other hand, declares it to be quite as brilliant as the second.
1759+023370, Ophiuchi (Σ 2272), 4 mag., and yellow, with a red companion, 6 mag., distance (Schiaparelli, 1882), 2.3″. Since Herschel's time, this companion has completed more than a full revolution round the primary. Period of revolution, 95 years. The parallax is 0.16″, the distance from the earth = 1,300,000 radii of the earth's orbit. The mean distance of the companion from its primary is 4,300 million kilometers, or nearly 30 radii of the earth's orbit, and the whole mass of the system is equal to about three times that of our sun.

1805+3100T Herculis, a variable star, discovered at Bonn in 1857. Its brilliancy ranges from 7 to 12 mag. It is of a deep red colour. Period 165 days.
1805+1627Σ 2289, Herculis, a star of 6 mag., with a companion 7 mag. Distance, only 1.2″.
1806-21364388, Star Cluster in Sagittarius, a fairly large, and scattered cluster of stars, ranging from 9.5 to 11 mag.
1806+06494390, Nebula in Ophiuchus, fairly brilliant, planetary. Its spectrum contains the three early recognised bright lines, of which the first is the most brilliant, the last (falling on F), the faintest.
1807-2105μ Sagittarii, a quintuple star, primary 3.5 mag., and yellowish, has a companion 10 mag., d = 25″, p = 119°; another 10 mag., d = 50″ p = 115°. The other two companions seen by Burnham, are merely faint points of light.
1809+795940, 41, Draconis (Σ 2308). Seen with the naked eye, these look like a star of 5 mag. They are really two stars 5.2 and 6 mag., distance 20″.
1811-18284397, Star Cluster in Sagittarius. A rich star cluster, discovered by Messier, 20 June, 1764. Nearly 1½° in diameter. Visible to the naked eye as an off-shoot of the Milky Way.
