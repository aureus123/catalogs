0510+2000Σ 674, Tauri, a star of 6.5 mag., with a companion 9 mag., distance 10.5″, angle of position 147.3°.
0511+3959λ Aurigae. 5 mag., with a companion 9 mag., distance 121″. Burnham found yet another very faint star at distance 40″, angle of position 198°.
0512+39131067, Star Cluster in Auriga, a beautiful group of stars slightly denser towards the centre. A star of 7 mag. outshines all the others. Discovered by Herschel, 18 Oct., 1786.
0512+4651Σ 681, Aurigae, a double star, primary 6.5 mag., companion 9 mag., distance 23.2″, angle of position 180.8°.
0512-0658τ Orionis. This star, 4 mag., has three very faint companions. Burnham gives the following measurements:— AB Distance 36.2″ Angle of position 249.1°. AC Distance 36.1″ Angle of position 60.0°. Bb Distance 4.0″ Angle of position 50.6°. It was Burnham who discovered that B is a double star: B is 10.5 mag., C 11 mag.
0517+0326m Orionis (Σ 696), a greenish-white star 5.5 mag., with a companion (white) 7 mag., distance 31.7″, angle of position 28.1°. No change of position has, as yet, been observed.
0517+1716111, Tauri, a star of 5.5 mag., with a companion 8 mag., distance 75″, angle of position 271°. Herschel had already made the measurements.
0517-2454Triple star in Lepus. The primary is 6 mag., the nearer companion which is at distance 3.5″, is 9 mag., the farther companion, which is at distance 59″, is 8.5 mag.
0517+33171101, Star Cluster in Auriga fairly large, and rich in stars, slightly denser towards the centre.
0518-0231η Orionis, 3.5 mag., with a companion 5 mag., at distance 110″. Dawes, in 1848, discovered that the primary was itself a double star, having a companion of 5.5 mag. at distance 1″. It requires a very strong instrument to "divide" this star.
0518+0614γ Orionis, a star of 2 mag., supposed by J. Herschel to be slightly variable. Gould, too, finds slight variations, but only of about ⅓ mag.
0519-24381112, Star Cluster in Lepus, a splendid globular cluster discovered by Méchain, and which Messier described as a starless nebula with a brilliant centre. Herschel was the first to resolve the nebula into stars.
0520+35131114, Star Cluster in Auriga, a somewhat crowded circular cluster, 4′ in diameter, consisting of small stars ranging in magnitude from 9 to 12. Discovered by W. Herschel, 17 Jan., 1787.
0521+0259ψ² Orionis, a star of 5.5 mag., with a very faint companion of 11 mag., distance 2.9″, angle of position 322°.
0521+35471119, Star Cluster in Auriga, a beautiful object, about 10′ in diameter. Discovered by Messier. A brilliant star may be observed in the centre. The stars are of unequal brilliancy. Its form, according to Messier, is quadratic. The cluster is not at all nebulous if examined with a powerful telescope.
