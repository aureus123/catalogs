1231+6009T Ursae Majoris, recognised as variable at Bonn in 1860. At maximum it is sometimes 7, though often only 8 mag.; at minimum under 13. Period 256 days. The star is of a reddish-yellow colour.
1232+12293121, Nebula in Virgo. Discovered by Messier in 1779, and described as a very faint nebula. John Herschel calls it brilliant, large, irregularly round, bright towards the centre, and probably capable of being resolved into stars.
1232+0739R Virginis, a reddish-yellow star, whose variability was recognised by Harding in 1809. At maximum it is 6.5 to 7.5 mag.; at minimum 10 to 11 mag. Period 146 days.
1233-26053128, Nebula in Hydra. Discovered by Messier in 1780, and described by him as a very faint starless nebula not easy to distinguish. Near it is a star of 6 mag. In 1786 the nebula was resolved into separate stars by F. W. Herschel's 20-foot reflector, these being very densely collected towards the centre. The cluster is 4′ long by 3′ broad.
1234-10583132, Nebula in Virgo. An elongated, elliptical nebula, brighter at the centre. With a low magnifying power several stars come into view at the same time as the nebula.
1235-1220Σ 1669, Virginis. A star of 6 mag., with a companion of nearly equal magnitude. Distance 5.8″.
1236-0047γ Virginis (Σ 1670), a yellowish star 3 mag., with a somewhat fainter companion. This latter is at present easy to observe (d = 5.2″, p = 336°, 1882): its period of revolution is 170 years. There is also another faint star (11.5 mag.), d = 103″, p = 80°.
1236+33123165, Nebula in Canes Venatici, a strip of nebula 12′ long, but faint, with a small star in the centre.
1238+02393176, Nebula in Virgo, somewhat faint, starless, brightest at the centre.
1238+12133182, Nebula in Virgo. Discovered by Messier, who, however, did not see a faint nebula which precedes it. Somewhat faint. As seen with the Leipsic refractor, it seems capable of being resolved into stars.
1239+6145S Ursae Majoris, of a reddish-yellow, recognised as variable by Pogson in 1853. The changes of brilliancy are somewhat irregular. At maximum it is 8, at minimum 10, sometimes 11 mag. Period 225 days, is apparently, slowly increasing.
1239+1502Σ 1678, Comae Berenices, 6 mag., with a companion 7 mag., distance 32″.
1242-05093227, Nebula in Virgo, fairly large, but not very brilliant, increasing in brilliancy towards the centre, with a kind of nucleus which Herschel thought resolvable into stars.
1245-0941Σ 1682, Virginis, 6.5 mag., yellowish, with a reddish companion 9 mag., d = 32″, p = 307°.
1245+41473258, Nebula in Canes Venatici. Discovered by Méchain, and described by him as a starless nebula, of fair brilliancy, 2.5′ in diameter, with brilliant centre. Several stars come into view at the same moment as this nebula, one of them a double star which follows it.
