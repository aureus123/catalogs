0603-11084, Monocerotis. A triple star; the nearer companion discovered by Burnham; the farther by Knott. Primary A is 6.5, B 9.5, C 10 mag. Measurements according to Dembowski, 1876.: AB distance 3.1″. Angle of position 178°. AC distance 8.9″. Angle of position 244.5°.
0606+05291376, Star Cluster in Orion, 5′ to 6′ in diameter, a rather crowded collection of brilliant and faint stars.
0607+12501383, Star Cluster in Pegasus, a beautiful cluster from 7′ to 8′ in diameter. With a low power, the field of vision seems filled with numerous stars of 10 to 12 mag. In two places the stars are densely crowded, and one of these places looks like nebula in a small telescope.
0607+3612Σ 872, Aurigae. Primary 6.5, companion 7 mag. The distance (11″) and angle of position (217°) do not seem to vary.
0608+2232η Geminorum. This yellow star, as discovered by Schmidt, in 1865, varies between 3.2 and 4 mag. Period of variability about 229 days.
0609-06145, Monocerotis, a star of 4.5 mag. of a decided orange colour.
0611+59254, Lyncis (Σ 881). This star, 6.5 mag., has a companion 8 mag., but this latter is so near to the primary that it can be divided only by a most powerful telescope. O. Struve (1847) found p = 95.6°, d = 0.87″.
0616+2234μ Geminorum. A yellow star 3 mag., with a very faint companion (11 mag or less) at distance 72″, angle of position 77°.
0616+58295, Lyncis (Σ 894), triple, primary 6, the nearer companion 10.5, the farther 8 mag. Burnham (1879) calculated: AB. p = 139.1°, d = 30.3″; AC: p = 272.5°, d = 95.9″.
0618+04398, Monocerotis (Σ 900). A yellowish star 4.5 mag., with a blueish companion 6.7 mag., distance 13.7″. Can be divided with a small telescope.
0619+0709T Monocerotis, A variable star, whose light fluctuates between 6.2 and 7.6 mag. within a period of 26.8 days. The changes of brilliancy were detected by Gould in 1871. According to Schoenfeld the maximum and minimum of brilliancy fluctuate somewhat at different periods.
0621+12431408, Star Cluster in Monoceros. Consists of 40 or 50 stars of from 10 to 11 mag., rather coarsely scattered upon a nebulous background. This last is difficult to see.
0621+205215, Geminorum, yellowish, 6.5 mag., with a blueish-white companion 7.5 mag., distance 31″.
0622-04411415, Star Cluster in Monoceros, contains a yellowish star 6 mag. (10 Monocerotis).
0623-065711, Monocerotis (Σ 919). Quadruple, A 5.5, B 6, C 6.5, D 12 mag. The three brilliant stars were first seen by Herschel, the faint companion by Burnham. Dunér (1871) calculates: AB Distance 7.2″ Angle of position 132.2°. BC Distance 2.3″ Angle of position 105.0°. Burnham: AD Distance 25.8″ Angle of position 56.1°.
