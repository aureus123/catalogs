0219+6652ι Cassiopeiae (Σ 262). A triple star, discovered by W. Herschel. The primary 4.2 mag. has one companion 7.1 mag., distance 1.5″, and a second companion 8.1 mag., distance 8.9″. The nearer companion is not easily distinguished.
0220-0043R Ceti, a variable star, first recognised as such by Argelander, 1866. At maximum it reaches sometimes 7 mag., at minimum, 13 mag. Period, 167 days.
0221-0141544, Planetary Nebula in Cetus, rather faint, with a diameter of from 3′ to 4′; there are several stars round it. Discovered by W. Herschel, 6 Jan., 1785.
0230+0503ν Ceti (Σ 281). A double star, 4.5 mag., with a faint companion of 9.5 mag. Distance, 7.7″; angle of position, 83°.
0230+240830, Arietis. A beautiful double star, not difficult to observe. Its primary is 6 mag.; companion, 6.5 mag.; distance, 38.6″. Both stars are white in colour. No change in position has been noticed since the time of Bradley.
0233+3833575, Nebula in Andromeda. Faint, 10′ in length, spindle-shaped, and surrounded by several faint stars.
0234+263333, Arietis (Σ 289). A double star, first seen by Herschel 27 Sept., 1779. Primary, 5.8 mag.; companion, 8.7 mag.; distance, 28.5″; angle of position, 359.4°. No change in position has been observed.
0235+4217584, Star Cluster in Perseus, having a diameter of 15′; discovered by Messier. The stars range from 9 to 11 mag., and are coarsely distributed; with a small telescope upwards of 100 stars can be distinguished.
0237-0031600, Nebula in Cetus, first discovered by Méchain in 1780. It is small, fairly brilliant, and is preceded by one star, and followed by two others. According to Lord Rosse the nebula shows spiral arrangement.
0237+0244γ Ceti (Σ 299). A star of yellow colour, 3.5 mag., with a companion 6.5 mag.; distance, 2.4″; angle of position, 286°.
0240-0804604, Nebula in Cetus. Faint, oval in shape, with a slight increase in brilliancy towards the centre; apparently a star cluster situated at an immeasurable distance.
0242+5524η Persei (Σ 307). 4 mag., with a companion (of blueish colour), 8 mag.; distance, 28″. There are also several very faint stars near it.
0242+1702T Arietis. A variable star, discovered by Auwers 1870. At maximum, 8 mag.; at minimum, 9.5 to 10 mag. Period, 324 days. Of a reddish colour.
0243+1658π Arietis (Σ 311). Triple; magnitudes, 5.5, 8, and 10; recognised as triple by Herschel in 1779. The nearest companion is at distance 3.3″: angle of position, 121°. The faint outer companion at distance 25″; angle of position, 110°.
0243+264541, Arietis. The primary is 4 mag. Herschel saw a faint companion in 1779 at distance 126″ (angle of position, as calculated by Burnham in 1879, 230°), then in 1782 a second at distance 34″, angle of position 203°. Finally, Struve, at Pulkowa, discovered yet another very faint companion (11 mag.) at distance 16″. The distance of this latter, as calculated by Burnham in 1879, was 21″, angle of position 266°.
