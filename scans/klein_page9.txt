0323+3653717, Star Cluster in Perseus, large (15′ in diameter), contains about 60 stars coarsely scattered. Discovered by Herschel, 28 Dec., 1799.
0324+5821Σ 396, Camelopardalis, a star 6.5 mag., with a faint companion, 8 mag., distance 20.3″.
0327+24047, Tauri (Σ 412), 6 mag., with a companion 10 mag., distance 22″. The star is thus described by W. Herschel, South and John Herschel. But Struve, with the Dorpat Refractor, divided the primary into two stars of almost equal brilliancy, at a distance of only 0.6″ from one another.
0331+0012Σ 422, Eridani, a double star, primary 6.5 mag., companion 8 mag., distance 6.5″. The primary is yellow, the companion blueish.
0334+0445Σ 430, Tauri. Triple 6.5, 9 and 9.5 mag. The first companion is at distance 26″, angle of position 55°. The second at distance 38″, angle of position 301°. The primary is yellowish.
0335+3335ο Persei (Σ 431) a star 5 mag., with a faint companion 9.5 mag., distance 20″.
0338+2320768, Nebula in the Pleiades, discovered by Tempel, 19 Oct., 1859. An extremely faint nebula, situated near the star Merope. The brothers Henry have discovered, by the aid of photography, another very faint nebula which seems to proceed from the star Maia, but this can only be seen with a most powerful telescope. (Cf. Sheet xiii.) * [* The fuller traces of nebulosity which, in 1885, appeared round Maia and Mérope, are now shown on more sensitive plates (Dec., 1887) as well-defined nebulæ of complex structure.—Ed.]
0340+2344η in the Pleiades, quadruple and a very beautiful object even as seen with a small telescope. The primary 3.5 mag. has one companion 6.5 mag. at distance 118″, angle of position 289°, a second 7 mag., distance 116″, angle of position 314°. Lastly there is a small star, 9 mag., at distance 115″ from the companion of 6.5 mag., angle of position 303°; η is also known by the name Alcyone. (See Sheet xiii.)
0340+5218775, Star Cluster in Perseus, small, consisting of about 30 stars, ranging in magnitude from 11 to 13, besides one star of 10 mag. The whole is from 2′ to 3′ in diameter.
0342+104630, Tauri (Σ 452) primary 5 mag., companion 9 mag., distance 9″.
0342+2341Σ 453, (Atlas) in the Pleiades, a star 4 mag. In 1827 and 1830 Struve fancied that he could distinguish a companion to this star of 8 mag., distance 0.8″. It was, however, never seen afterwards by Struve or any other observer. Even Burnham always found the star to be single and perfectly round.
0347+3132ζ Persei (Σ 464). Quintuple. The primary 2.7 mag. has, at distance 13″ a companion 9.5 mag., at distance 90″ another of 10 mag, and a third 10 mag., at distance 122″. There is besides a faint star of 12 mag., distance 33″, angle of position 286°.
0348-031932, Eridani (Σ 470), primary 5 mag., yellowish, with a companion 6 mag., distance 6.7″, of a blueish colour. Discovered by W. Herschel in 1781.
