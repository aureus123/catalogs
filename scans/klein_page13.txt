0501-03311005, Nebula in Orion, fairly brilliant, 3′ in diameter, and gradually fading towards the edges. It forms a triangle with two stars, 10 mag. and 12 mag. respectively. Discovered by Herschel, 2 Jan., 1786.
0501+0821ι Orionis. A star of 6 mag., with a companion 7 mag., at distance 1.1″, angle of position 205°.
0502+2752Σ 645, Aurigae. A double star, primary 6.5 mag., companion 8 mag., distance 12″, angle of position 26.8°.
0503+7905Σ 634, Camelopardalis. A star of 4.5 mag., with a companion 8 mag. In 1834 the distance was 34.5″, but in 1880 it was only 20″; angle of position 60°.
0503+16321030, Star Cluster in Taurus, 20′ to 25′ in diameter, rich and fairly crowded with stars ranging from 11 mag. to 14 mag. Discovered by Herschel, 19 Feb., 1784.
0505+0053Σ 652, Orionis. A double star, primary 6.5 mag., companion 8 mag., distance only 1.7″, angle of position (Struve, 1830) 184°.
0507+0243ρ Orionis (Σ 654). A yellow star, 4.7 mag., with a companion 8.5 mag., of a blueish white; distance 7″. No change has been observed in the position of the companion since Herschel's time.
0507-1201ι Leporis (Σ 655). A greenish star, 5 mag., with a faint companion 10.5 mag; distance 12.8″. This distance does not seem to vary. The angle of position, 337°, also seems to be nearly invariable.
0508-5327R Aurigae. A variable star, first recognised as such at Bonn Observatory in 1862. At maximum it reaches 6.5 to 7.5 mag., at minimum it is almost 13. Period 465 days. The changes in brilliancy are sometimes very peculiar.
0508+4552α Aurigae (Capella). A white star, 1 mag., with companion 9 mag; distance 159,″ angle of position 146.2° (discovered by Herschel). Burnham found two other very faint companions, magnitude 12 and 13 mag., distance 78″ and 126″, angle of position 317° and 183° respectively. Struve (in 1838) conjectured that Capella had become more brilliant than formerly, and that it was at one time fainter than Vega. J. Herschel supported this theory.
0508+323314, Aurigae (Σ 652), triple. The primary, 5.5 mag., has a companion 7 mag., at distance 14.5″. This was first seen by W. Herschel, 24 Sept., 1780. Struve, in 1830, discovered another very faint companion (11 mag.) at distance 12.6″, angle of position 342°.
0508-1305κ Leporis (Σ 661). A double star, primary 5, companion 8 mag., distance 2.5″. According to Struve, the primary is yellowish, the companion blueish.
0509+0820β Orionis (Rigel) (Σ 668). A brilliant star, 1 mag., with a companion 8 mag., distance 9.6″. Discovered by W. Herschel, and a very beautiful object in a powerful telescope. The companion, as remarked by Burnham in 1871, is itself a double star, but it is such a difficult object that Burnham himself, with the 18-inch refractor at Chicago, only at times succeeded in "dividing" it. Other observers have so far been unable to do so. There is yet another very faint star at distance 44″, angle of position 1.5°. According to Seidel, β is in a slight degree variable.
