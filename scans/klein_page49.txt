1829-24004424, Star Cluster in Sagittarius, discovered by Abraham Ihle, 1665. Messier could not distinguish any star in it, catalogued it as circular, and easily seen with a common telescope of 3½′ focus. It is surrounded by 5 irregularly placed stars. Herschel's 10-foot telescope resolved the nebula into a star cluster. Towards the middle the stars are denser; diameter 8′.
1830-08194426, Star Cluster in Scutum Sobieski, large, fairly rich, and of moderate density. Discovered by W. Herschel, 16 June, 1784.
1833+3840α Lyrae (Vega). This splendid star, 1 mag., has near it several faint stars, but their connection with it is merely optical. One of them, which was used by Struve and Brünnow as a comparison star for calculating the parallax of Vega, is of 9.5 mag, d = 48″, p = 156°, another of 9 mag., d = 150″, p = 40°. Winnecke and Burnham have also measured a very faint star (13 mag.), d = 52″, p = 292°.
1835-04524429, Star Cluster in Scutum Sobieski, large and rich, but the stars are little more than minute luminous points.
1839-09314432, Star Cluster in Scutum Sobieski. Discovered by Messier, 2′ in diameter.
1840-01055, Aquilae (Σ 2379), 6 mag., with a blueish companion 7.4 mag., distance 13.2″. There is also a second companion 11 mag., d = 27.3″, p = 145°.
1840+3933ε and 5 Lyrae. A very interesting pair of double stars, first observed by Flamsteed, though with keen sight and under specially favourable circumstances, ε and 5 Lyrae can be seen as clearly separated. The distance, according to Auwers, 1862, is 208″. The fact that each of these stars is itself double was verified by Christian Mayer, and in 1823 John Herschel discovered three other very faint stars between ε and 5 Lyrae. The distance of the two components from ε is, according to Dembowski, 1863, 3.045″. The distance of both stars from 5 Lyrae was in the same year 2.48″, and seems to be slowly decreasing.
1841+3729ζ Lyrae, a star of 4 mag., with a companion 5.5 mag.; distance 44″. Recognised by Flamsteed.
1841-0550R Scuti Sobieski. Recognised as variable by Pigott in 1795. The change of brilliancy is very irregular; at maximum the star is sometimes 4.5 mag., though it is often under 5.5 mag., at minimum it is 6, sometimes 8 mag. The period is irregular.
1841+2026110, Herculis, 4 mag., with a companion 11 mag., d = 61″, p = 92°. Burnham saw a still fainter companion, d = 45″, p = 95°.
1843+6056Σ 2403, Draconis, 6 mag., with a companion 9 mag. According to Struve, in 1832, d = 1.9″, p = 259°.
