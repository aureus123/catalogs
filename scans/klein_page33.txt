1220+13362961, Nebula in Virgo. Comes into sight at the same moment as the nebula 2930, brilliant, round, with nucleus in the centre. Also discovered by Messier, 3′ to 4′ in diameter.
1221+31532972, Nebula in Coma Berenices, fairly brilliant and large, increasing in brightness towards the centre, at which there is a star.
1222+44453002, Nebula in Canes Venatici, fairly brilliant and large, was resolved into stars by Herschel's great telescope.
1223+263417, Comae Berenices. A star of 5.5 mag., with a companion 6 mag., distance 145″.
1224+08403021, Nebula in Virgo. Discovered by Oriani in 1771, and described by Messier as a nebula, which can be seen but with difficulty in a 3½-foot telescope. On right and left is a star of 6 mag. An insignificant object in small instruments.
1224+42183041, 3042, Nebula in Canes Venatici. A double nebula. The preceding one is faint, fairly small, and irregular in shape, the other which follows 3′ to the south is larger and more brilliant. In Herschel's telescope it was partly resolvable into stars.
1224-1551δ Corvi 2.5 mag. with a companion 8 mag., distance 23″, angle of position 214°.
1225+13033035, Nebula in Virgo. Discovered by Messier, of moderate brilliancy, round and large, brightest in the centre.
1225+26263043, Nebula in Coma Berenices, faint, round, with nucleus.
1226+15053049, Nebula in Virgo. Discovered by Messier in 1781, and described as a starless nebula, situated between two stars of 6 mag., which come into view at the same moment. It is elliptical, and the northern part is brighter than the southern. Lassell and Vogel have made maps of this nebula.
1228+08213075, Nebula in Virgo, of moderate brilliancy, with a sudden increase of brightness towards the centre. Preceded by a star of 9 mag.
1229+190224, Comae Berenicies (Σ 1657), 5 mag. and yellow, with a blueish companion 6 mag. Distance (1863) 20″, angle of position 271°.
1230+13133097, Nebula in Virgo. Discovered as a very faint starless nebula by Messier; it is round and brighter towards the centre. Vogel observed it with the Leipsic Refractor, and describes it as very brilliant 2′ in extent, suddenly denser at the centre, with a brilliant nucleus.
1230+28373101, Nebula in Coma Berenices. Fairly bright and large, gradually more brilliant towards the centre. Three stars follow it.
1230+12063105, Nebula in Virgo, a faint, double nebula, coming into view at the same moment as two others still fainter. Tempel has made a map of the whole with the neighbouring stars.
1230+26393106, Nebula in Coma Berenices, a somewhat faint nebula, very large, 3-4′ in breadth, brighter towards the centre, with a nucleus like a star of 10 mag. Preceded by four faint stars.
