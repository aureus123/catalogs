0350+3940ε Persei (Σ 471), primary 3.5 mag., companion 8 mag., distance 9″. The companion is probably variable, its colour too, is said to change from blue to red.
0350+8022Σ 460, Cephei, a very close double star, primary, 5.5 mag., companion 6 mag., distance only 0.7″.
0354+1209λ Tauri, found to be variable by Baxendell in 1848. It belongs to the Algoltype. Period 4 days; the changes in brilliancy, however, only occupy 10ʰ; magnitude at maximum 3.4, at minimum 4.2. It loses more quickly than it gains in brilliancy.
0357+6031801, Nebula in Camelopardalis. Rosse saw a star of 14 mag., surrounded by a nebular ring in the centre of this nebula. To observe this nebula in detail a most powerful telescope is required: even when examined with an 8-inch refractor it is very faint.
0357+6202Σ 485, Camelopardalis. Two stars of about 6 mag. at 18″ distance. A triple star (Σ 484) consisting of three stars of 9 mag., precedes this double star.
0401+4912809, Star Cluster in Perseus, very beautiful and rich in stars, 7′ in diameter, rather crowded and irregularly round in shape. This cluster is surrounded by a ring of faint stars, which cannot be well seen except with a very powerful instrument.
0401+1450Σ 495, Tauri. Primary 6.5 mag., companion 9 mag., distance 4″.
0402+3028810, Nebulous Star in Taurus, a star of 8 mag., surrounded by a faint nebula 3′ in diameter.
0406+4806μ Persei. Discovered to be a double star by W. Herschel, 2 Aug., 1782. The primary is 4 mag. and white in colour, the companion 8.5 mag. is at distance 92″. At Pulkowa another very faint companion was discovered, distance (according to Burnham) 14″, angle of position 349°.
0406+5054820, Star Cluster in Perseus, rich in stars, nearly 15′ in diameter, rather crowded with a number of fairly brilliant stars, apparently arranged in curves; 30′ to the south it is followed by a reddish-coloured star of 9 mag.
0408+085847, Tauri. Primary 5 mag., companion 8 mag., distance only 0.8″. There is, in addition, a second very faint companion (12 mag.) at distance 32.2″, angle of position 223°. This last was discovered by Burnham.
0409+15064, Tauri. Up to 1872, J. Schmidt found that he could always make this star out with the naked eye as of 6 mag. In September, 1872, it decreased to 7 mag., and then again became visible to the naked eye, nor has any variation been observed in its brilliancy since 1874.
0409-1033A Eridani (Σ 516), primary 5 mag., and yellowish in colour, with a very faint companion 9 mag., distance 6.3″. First discovered to be a double star by W. Herschel, 31 Jan., 1785.
0409-1302826, Nebula in Eridanus, a planetary disk, of fair brilliancy. According to Lassell there is a star of 12 mag. in the centre. Herschel who discovered it in 1785, took it to be a star cluster, and its spectrum supports this view. The nebula is a very small one.
