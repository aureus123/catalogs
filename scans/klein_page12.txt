0430+53152, Camelopardalis (Σ 566). 6 mag., yellowish, with a blueish companion, 7.4 mag.; distance (according to Engelmann, 1883), 1.9″; angle of position, 291.9°.
0432+1541σ Tauri. Two stars of 4.6 and 5.5 mag.; distance, 429″; can be divided by the naked eye.
0435+2244τ Tauri. 4.5 mag., with an easily distinguished companion, 7 mag., at distance 63″. Discovered by Chr. Mayer.
0438-090155, Eridani (Σ 590). A star of 6.2 mag., with a companion, 7 mag.; distance, 9″. The primary is yellowish, the companion white in colour. Found to be double by W. Herschel in 1783.
0441+1043905, Star Cluster in Orion, consisting of 18 stars of from 8 to 11 mag. Somewhat scattered. Vogel has measured their relative positions with the micrometer.
0445+1720V Tauri. A variable star, first recognised as such by Auwers, 1870. The star is reddish. At maximum it reaches 8.3 mag.; at minimum it diminishes to 13 mag. Period, 168.6 days.
0448+53337, Camelopardalis (Σ 610). A star of 5 mag., with a very faint companion (11.3 mag.), at distance 25″. Dembowski, in 1864, discovered that the primary was itself a double star, having a companion of 8 mag. at distance 1.2″.
0450-0522b Eridani, 6 mag., with companion 8 mag., distance 64″.
0451+37434, Aurigae (Σ 616). A star 5.5 mag., giving a greenish light, with a companion 7 mag., distance 6″. First recognised as a double star by W. Herschel, 30 Oct. 1779.
0452+0757R Orionis. A variable star, of a deep red colour, discovered by Hind in 1848. At maximum it reaches almost 9 mag., at minimum it is less than 13. Period, 379 days. According to Schoenfeld the increase in brilliancy from 10 mag. upwards occupies 70 days; the decrease from maximum to 10 mag., 105 days.
0452+5341940, Star Cluster in Camelopardalis, fairly large and conspicuous. The stars are of various magnitudes, and are closer together towards the centre.
0453+4339ε Aurigae. Recognised as variable by Schmidt and Heis. The star is 3 mag., but has been seen at times as a star of 4.5 mag. The variations of brilliancy are, however, very irregular, and are often for a long time imperceptible.
0454-1459R Leporis. A variable star of a bright red colour, called a "crimson star" by Hind. Schmidt, in 1855, was the first to recognise it as variable. The period is about 438 days, but seems to be very irregular. At maximum the star is 6 to 7, at minimum 8.5 mag. It has a remarkable spectrum.
0454+0326Σ 627, Orionis, 6.5 mag., with a companion of nearly 7 mag., at distance 21″. This latter has not undergone any perceptible change since 1831.
0459+010196 B., Orionis. A remarkable red-coloured star, estimated by Lalande and Bessel as of 7 mag., but by Birmingham, in 1871, as of 6 mag. The red tint is also probably slightly variable.
0500+3712996, Star Cluster in Auriga. This is really not a cluster, but rather a rich field of stars. Herschel first saw it 17 Jan., 1787. The most brilliant stars contained in it are of 7.5 mag., the faintest 11.5 mag.
