0252+2052ε Arietis (Σ 333), a double star, primary 4.5 mag., companion 6 mag., distance 1.5″. As conjectured by Struve, the primary probably varies from 4.5 mag. to 6.5 mag., and the companion also seemed to him variable. Engelmann supports this theory.
0257+3823ρ Persei. This orange-coloured star was first recognised as variable in 1854, by J. Schmidt. The period is seemingly quite irregular, and the brightness at maximum varies considerably.
0258+244752, Arietis (Σ 346), triple, but cannot be clearly divided except by a very powerful telescope. The primary, 5.5 mag., has one companion at distance 0.7″; the outer companion, which is very faint (11 mag.), is at distance 5″.
0300+4030β Persei (Algol), one of the most interesting variable stars. The variation was discovered as far back as 1767 or 1769, by Montanari, but Goodricke, in 1782, was the first to recognise its true nature. According to his account, the star remains invariable for 2½ days, and then decreases in 4½ hours to 3.7 mag., and during the next 4½ hours it increases to 2.2 mag. The period is 2 days 20h. 48m. 53.4s. This is not constant, there being a difference of a few seconds in the course of the year.
0307-013994, Ceti, 5.5 mag., has a very faint companion at distance 5.7″, which it needs an extremely powerful telescope to distinguish.
0307+4647658, Star Cluster in Perseus. A beautiful cluster 8′ in diameter, very rich in stars of 10 mag. and under. Discovered by W. Herschel, 27 Dec., 1786.
0307-292812, Eridani, a star of 3.5 mag., with a companion 7.5 mag., distance 2.4″, angle of position 316°.
0307+651352, Camelopardalis. A star of 6.5 mag., with a companion 7 mag., at a distance of only 0.5″. A very difficult object.
0309+4002Σ 369, Double Star in Perseus. The primary, 7 mag., is of a yellowish-white colour; the companion, 8 mag., is blueish-white. The distance, 3.5″, seems to be increasing, but very gradually.
0316-1549692, Nebula in Eridanus, rather faint, large, irregularly round in shape, gradually increasing in brilliancy towards the centre. A star, 7 mag., precedes the nebula.
0319+5931Σ 385, Camelopardalis. The primary, 5 mag., is yellow in colour; the companion, 9 mag., distance 2.4″, angle of position 161°, is white.
0321+5502Σ 390, Camelopardalis. Primary 5 mag., greenish-white in colour, with a faint companion 9.5 mag., distance 15″, angle of position 160°.
0321-2146709, Nebula in Eridanus, discovered by W. Herschel; fairly bright, elongated, but small, with a nucleus, followed southwards by a small and very faint nebula.
0322+3515R Persei, a variable star. At maximum it sometimes reaches as much as 8 mag., and at minimum becomes frequently less than 13 mag. Period, 208.5 days. According to Schoenfeld it remains at 12 mag. and under for two months' time.
